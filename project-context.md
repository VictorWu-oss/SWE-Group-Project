# Project Context: Cal Poly Bathroom Finder

## Product
A mobile system for finding bathrooms on Cal Poly SLO’s campus and viewing community ratings for cleanliness and busyness.

## Users
- Students: find a nearby bathroom that is clean and not too busy.
- Faculty and staff: find available bathrooms while working or moving around campus.
- Visitors: locate bathrooms in unfamiliar campus buildings.
- Administrators or facilities staff: identify bathrooms that may need more cleaning or attention.

## Evidence
- Students may not know where bathrooms are located in every building.
- Users may want to avoid bathrooms that are crowded or have long lines.
- Cleanliness can vary between bathrooms and throughout the day.
- Facilities staff may benefit from seeing patterns in reported cleanliness issues.

## Confirmed decisions
- Users can search for bathrooms on the Cal Poly SLO campus.
- Each bathroom can have a cleanliness rating.
- Each bathroom can have a busyness/activity rating.
- The system should show a bathroom’s location.
- Ratings are based on user reports.

## Constraints
- Do not collect or display users’ personal information with their ratings.
- Users should not submit inappropriate or harmful comments.
- Ratings may be subjective and should be treated as user-reported information.
- The app should only include bathrooms that are available to the public or appropriate campus users.

## Open questions
- Will users need to sign in before submitting a rating?
- Will ratings use a 1–5 scale, labels such as “clean” and “dirty,” or both?
- How long should a busyness rating remain visible before it becomes outdated?
- Can users leave written comments, or only ratings?
- Will the app include accessibility information, such as wheelchair-accessible bathrooms?
- Who verifies that bathroom locations and building information are accurate?

## Task prompt pattern
Using only the evidence and confirmed decisions above:

1. Draft one user story for a named user.
2. Draft Given / When / Then acceptance criteria.
3. List assumptions separately.
4. List questions that require a stakeholder decision.
5. Do not invent policy, timing, privacy, or moderation requirements.
